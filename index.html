The Legend

Slingers is a fast-action shooter game for 3–8 players. The frontier is unforgiving—only the smartest, fastest, and most cunning Slingers survive the deadly duels that forge legends.

Choose from 12 unique Slingers, each with abilities that shape your strategy. Navigate the grid-based map, manage your weapon and equipment loadouts, mount trusty steeds, and use action and combat cards to outgun your opponents.

Every location offers new opportunities. Every card drawn could change your fate. Every die roll could be your last.

Game Overview:
  • Move across the grid-based game board
  • Each player starts with 99 health (choose lower for a faster game)
  • Start with 5 cards and draw an additional card each turn
  • Every turn ends with a combat phase where two players "duel"
  • Weapon damage depends on how far you are from your target
  • Reach 0 health and you are eliminated—be the last Slinger standing!
3–8 Players

How to Play

Master the cards, control the board, survive the duel

Game Setup

1

Choose Your Slinger

Each player chooses a character and takes their player piece and character life tracker

2

Prepare the Board

Place the 5 location boards anywhere on the map so their grids line up with the grid of the game board

3

Determine Turn Order

Roll a six-sided die to decide who goes first

4

Deal Starting Hands

Deal each player 5 cards. Everyone starts with 99 health (choose lower for a faster game). Illness cards dealt to a starting hand are discarded and replaced

5

Place Characters

First player places their piece anywhere on the map, then clockwise each player does the same. You cannot start inside a building or within 3 squares of another player

6

Begin the Duel!

You're ready to face off in the ultimate Slinger showdown

Card Types

Action Cards

Use during your turn before declaring attack phase. Play as many as you want to set up your strategy.

Weapons

Equip during setup or your turn. Bring weapons back into your hand or swap them out at any point before you declare attack phase. Every weapon is melee, single wield, or dual wield and lists its damage at close, medium, and long range.

Equipment

Equip or unequip during setup or your turn, any time before attack phase. You may only have 2 equipped at once, and you cannot equip 2 of the same equipment card.

Combat Cards

Play during attack phase as the attacker or the defender, before the attack roll is rolled. Play as many as you'd like—perfect for turning the tables!

Steeds

Kept as a separate face-down STEED DECK. You can only have one steed at a time, in hand or equipped. An equipped steed takes damage before you do—if it dies, leftover damage carries through to you and the steed goes to the bottom of the steed deck.

Pets

Equipped just like equipment cards, during setup or your turn before attack phase—you can equip as many pets as you want. Pet abilities activate each turn. Pets have their own health and must be targeted separately from their owner; the attacker chooses how to split damage among them, so multiple pets can be hit in one attack. At 0 health a pet is discarded.

Health Cards

Play at any time to heal damage except during attack phase (even in jail!). You cannot heal past your starting maximum health. Some health cards have extra abilities, such as negating illness cards or healing steeds.

Illness Cards

Must be revealed as soon as you draw them, and the negative effects apply to whoever drew the card. If dealt to your starting hand, discard and draw a new card. Illness cards can never be given away or taken by another player by any means.

Money

Place money cards face up in front of you. Collect $1,000 and reach the Trading Post to pay off your bounty and win the game. Eliminate a player (or send them to jail) and you take all their money. Money can be traded or discarded like a normal card, but because it never sits in your hand it's safe from cards and abilities that steal from a player's hand.

Weapon Types

Single Wield

Must be your only equipped weapon.

Dual Wield

You can have 2 equipped at once (or 1 dual wield and 1 melee).

Melee

You can have 2 equipped at once (or 1 melee and 1 dual wield). A melee weapon can be thrown from any distance—if thrown, the opponent takes 1 hit of close range damage and adds the melee weapon to their hand.

Range Tiers

Every weapon has three range tiers. Distance decides which tier you're firing in—and how much damage you deal. Multiply your attack roll by the weapon's damage at that range.

Close

Usually 0–1 squares away

Medium

Usually 2–3 squares away

Long

Usually 4–5 squares away

Card Attributes

Speed

Steed attribute. Multiply your movement roll by this number to determine your total movement. Higher speed = greater mobility!

Trample

Steed attribute. The damage your steed does to a player you jump over during movement (you still cannot finish your movement in an occupied space).

Health

The same icon is used on health cards and for steed and pet health. A steed takes damage before you do; pets are attacked separately.

Taking Your Turn

1

Draw

Start your turn by drawing a card from the deck.

→
2

Action

Play action cards and swap equipped cards. Equip or unequip weapons, equipment, pets, and steeds, and use equipment and pet abilities.

→
3

Move

Roll the 4-sided movement die and move according to the movement rules.

→
4

Action

Play more action cards and swap equipped cards. You can keep taking action steps right up until you declare attack phase.

→
5

Attack

Select a target and attack! Allow them time to play combat cards, then roll for the attack. Your turn ends after attack phase.

→
6

End Turn

At the end of your turn you must discard down to 6 or less cards.

Important Notes:

  • Every turn normally starts by drawing a card and ends after attack phase
  • Most cards, other than combat cards, need to be played before attack phase
  • Movement can happen any time during your turn before attack phase is initiated
  • Hand limit is 6 cards at the end of your turn
  • If you want a more symmetrical game, you may play without character abilities

Attacking - The Duel

Attacking in Slingers works like a duel. Even if you are the "attacker," you are also being attacked by your target.

1

Declare Target

Initiate attack phase by selecting another player—or that player's pets—to attack. Before rolling the dice, allow time for the opponent to play any combat cards, and play your own combat cards during this time.

2

Roll the Dice

Roll 1 die for each weapon involved in the combat. The attacker gets the higher roll(s) and the defender gets the lower roll(s).

3

Determine Distance

Distance is the number of squares BETWEEN you and the target on the shortest path—take the larger of the horizontal and vertical distance. That distance sets the range tier each weapon is firing in.

4

Calculate Damage

Look up the damage each weapon does at its current range tier on the weapon's damage table, apply any modifiers to the attack rolls, then multiply.

Damage Calculation Formula

Step 1: Find the Range Tier

Distance to target → CLOSE (0-1), MEDIUM (2-3), or LONG (4-5)

Step 2: Read the Damage Table

Weapon Damage Table + Range Tier = Damage Per Hit

Step 3: Apply Modifiers to the Roll

Attack Roll + Modifiers = Final Roll (modifiers apply to all weapons!)

Step 4: Total Damage

Final Roll × Damage Per Hit = Total Damage

Step 5: Apply Damage

Subtract total damage from the target's health

Combat Notes:

  • Modifiers apply to all weapons involved in the attack
  • With multiple weapons, assign your dice rolls to each weapon to your liking
  • Distance is measured BETWEEN players—if you can move 1 square to occupy the same space as another player, the distance is 0
  • Punching: if you have no weapon equipped you can still punch. This does 1 damage at close range and 0 at all other ranges
  • Pets must be attacked separately from their owner. When attacking pets, the attacker chooses how to allocate the damage, so multiple pets can be damaged in 1 attack
  • An equipped steed takes damage before the player. If the steed dies, leftover damage applies to the player who rode it

Movement

1. Roll for Movement

Roll the 4-sided die to get your base movement roll. Movement can happen any time during your turn before attack phase is initiated.

2. Apply Modifiers

Apply any movement modifiers—any cards or abilities that are not steed related, such as the +2 from "Lucky Boots".

3. Multiply by Steed Speed

Finally, multiply that number by the SPEED of your equipped steed, if you have one. This is the total number of spaces you can move this turn.

Free Direction

You can move in any direction—vertical, horizontal, or diagonal—and change direction during movement. You do not need to move your entire movement roll.

Movement Restrictions

You cannot move through or land in occupied spaces. The exception is a steed with TRAMPLE, which can move through an occupied space and inflict trample damage on that player—though you still cannot finish your movement there.

Winning the Game

Last Slinger Standing

To win the game, you must be the last person standing. When a player's health hits 0, they are no longer in the game.

Pay Off Your Bounty

You can also win the game by collecting $1,000 and returning to the Trading Post to pay off your bounty.

Tie Game

A game can end in a tie if the last 2 players' health hit 0 during the final attack (attack target's damage is still applied to the attacker even if the target's health hits 0).

Death

1

Player Eliminated

When a player's health hits 0, they are out of the game. Remove their game piece from the board.

2

Loot the Corpse

Starting with the player who dealt the finishing blow and continuing in the order of play, each player can select 1 card from the dead player's cards (equipped cards as well as cards from their hand).

3

Collect the Money

When you eliminate another player, you collect all of their money. When you send someone to jail, you also collect all of their money unless they avoid jail.

4

Clean Up

Discard any extra cards from the dead player. Make sure every player discards back down to 6 or less cards.

Locations on the Map

Strategic buildings offer unique advantages and restrictions

Jail

  • A player may only enter jail if sent there by a card
  • While in jail, you can only use health cards or cards that can be used to get out of jail
  • Get out of jail cards must be used BEFORE being sent to jail to avoid your sentence
  • Cards that change your location (e.g., "Last Call") can get you out of jail if used by another player
  • You can be attacked while in jail, but you do not counterattack
  • After the player who sent you to jail completes their next turn, your sentence is over and you must move 1 square to leave the jail

Trading Post

  • Players can freely enter and leave the Trading Post
  • During your turn, if in the Trading Post, you may trade cards with other players in the Trading Post
  • No combat is allowed involving players in the Trading Post
  • If you spend more than 5 turns in the Trading Post, you must leave and are not allowed back in the Trading Post
  • Return here with $1,000 to pay off your bounty and win the game

Saloon

  • Players can freely enter and leave the Saloon
  • While in the Saloon you may discard as many cards as you would like and receive 5 health for each card you discard. You can only do this during your turn
  • Players in the Saloon can only attack and be attacked by other players in the Saloon

Stables

  • Players can freely enter and leave the Stables
  • During your turn, if in the Stables, you may discard a card to draw the top card from the STEED DECK
  • All combat is allowed into, out of, and in the Stables

Jack's Firearm Emporium

  • Players can freely enter and leave Jack's
  • During your turn, if in Jack's, you may discard 3 cards to go through the deck and add a WEAPON of your choice to your hand
  • All combat is allowed into, out of, and in Jack's

Confusing Cards

There's only so much room on a card, so some descriptions can be confusing. Here's a little extra clarity.

Lucky Hat

The "hat hand" gets passed to the other player in the event "Lucky Hat" is stolen. Both hands get combined when you die for looting purposes, so the "hat hand" is reset on death.

Bounty

The player who plays "Bounty" does NOT attempt an attack on the chosen player. Only the other players attempt the attack.

Double Barrel Shotgun

The "Double Barrel Shotgun" shoots 2 shells. Roll a 5 or above and they both hit for 40 damage. A 3 or 4 is 20 damage. Below a 3 is 0 damage.

The Slingers

Twelve unique gunslingers, each with their own story, style, and reason to fight

Tap any character card to reveal their special abilities

Menk

A young wannabe hero—it's no surprise. He was told his entire life his dad was a true war hero, how can a guy not idolize something like that. He wears his father's gear like some kinda ghost of justice. Truth be told, he's a good shot and he's killed before—defending a friend.

Menk

Cunning Dodge

Whenever in combat, roll a 6-sided die:

1-4: nothing
5+: dodge full attack and draw a card

Daliore

Not gonna lie, she's kind of a psycho. One minute she's a good flirt and drinking buddy, next second she's got two revolvers pressed to your temple. You can't predict what you'll do that will send her off into a rampage. They used to send her in alone to clear out enemy strongholds—she had a better temper then.

Daliore

Special Ability

No limit on equipment cards

Once per turn: discard a card to roll a 6-sided die:
1-3: draw a card
4-5: draw 2 cards
6: draw 3 cards

Sabja

In a land of gunpowder and steel, Sabja is an anomaly. A silent warrior who prefers to speak with his fists. His origins are as mysterious as the desert mirages, but his deadly, dance-like fighting style can disarm a gunslinger in the blink of an eye. He is a phantom of the frontier, a living testament that the quickest hand in the West doesn't always belong to a gunman.

Sabja

Special Ability

Cannot be hit if distance is 0

+1 to attack roll for each melee weapon

Punches do 3 damage

Ruby

The young barkeep—quite the firecracker for such a small little miss. Her Pa used to run the good saloon, 'til he got himself into some trouble in a card game with some circus folk, left all the duties of the watering hole to his only daughter. Ruby won't start the fight, but she'll sure finish it, especially to protect her saloon and her town.

Ruby

Special Ability

When in the saloon:
+2 to attack roll
Discard a card for 10 health

When "Last Call" is played, go through the deck and add 1 card to your hand

Noises

Another mystery. Sure, he's a talkative enough fella, lots of brief words of wisdom to give, but I'm not sure even he remembers his past. Many pin him to be a "soul walker" or some kinda shaman, and who can blame them? The dude walks blindfolded with no guide, and he knows exactly where to look when he wants to talk to you—a little spooky.

Noises

Special Ability

Once per turn, you can re-roll any dice

If attack roll, including modifiers, is 6+: use close range damage

Fhangs

He's one of those Lonely Wanderer type fellas, hiking town to town, hobble to hobble, mostly getting by on his own two feet. This tall son of a gun is not too keen to being questioned, he don't really talk much to begin with. He walks like an upright frog with his massive grit covered shotgun slung on his shoulder, and he will rip your torso in half without batting an eye.

Fhangs

Special Ability

Can use "Double Barrel Shotgun" from up to 5 squares away

+2 to attack roll with "Double Barrel Shotgun"

+2 to movement roll without "Double Barrel Shotgun"

Fereen

The boss lady—a truly menacing figure. Her prissy outfit is always pristine and starchy and if you cause even a spec of stain to fall upon her person, you will be met with adversity like you've never seen before. She is an owner, a proprietor and a fierce opponent to any who try to muddy up her prim and pristine operations.

Fereen

Special Ability

If you deal 15+ damage during combat, steal a random card from the opponent's hand

If the player has money you may steal a cash card instead

Discard any card to avoid jail

Ghala

An agent, living as a soldier of fortune. She's spent her life as a warrior for her own purposes, mainly as a means to survive. Contracts and bloodshed mean shelter and a warm meal. Connection is not worth her time and her collection of books picked off corpses is ever growing.

Ghala

Special Ability

+1 squares to each range tier

+1 to attack roll when wielding a single weapon

Panch

You gotta love this guy. Don't speak a lick of English, but if he sizes you up to be a good fella, he'll be your friend till the end of time. A true enjoyer of life, he wears the flashiest clothing his daughters can create and loves nothing more than to stop and have a meal with companions.

Panch

Special Ability

No hand limit

-2 to movement roll

-2 to opponent's attack roll

Treech

A slippery little blood sucker. He may act tough with his big sister standing besides him, but this greasy con man is as two-faced and cowardly as they come. That crooked smile is always just to distract you from whatever twisted thing he's planning.

Treech

Special Ability

Draw an additional card each turn

You may take movement step before AND after attack phase

Boone

As an orphan, he grew up living in the stables as the stable boy. Sleeping every night among the horses, giving him a special bond with all the steeds in town. When his Trusty Steed got sick, he robbed a train passing through town to afford medicine since the owner thought the horse was too old anyways. Get him a mount and he is unstoppable.

Boone

Special Ability

When mounted on a steed:
+1 to your steed
+1 to attack roll

Choose from the top 3 cards when drawing from the steed deck

Sage

Growing up with Pancha as her father was not always easy. She tended to find more love with animals than her family. Now grown up, her and her bird, Canyon, silently roam the desert from town to town punishing anyone who brings harm to other creatures. Currently wanted for killing a group of hunters.

Sage

Special Ability

When in combat: if opponent uses a combat card, choose 1 for Canyon to counter

Pet abilities activate twice
